Topics covered

No prior background in blockchain is required.

Decentralisation and its foundations

The double-spending problem, how Bitcoin addressed it without a trusted intermediary, and how Ethereum extended the ledger into a programmable platform.

Proof of Stake and the Merge

Validators, slots and finality, and Ethereum's 2022 transition from Proof of Work to Proof of Stake on a live network.

Open research problems

Goals set out in the Ethereum Foundation's Strawmap: faster finality, greater capacity, fair transaction ordering, quantum safety and privacy.

Security and financial loss

Case studies including the 2025 Bybit theft and the 2016 DAO exploit, and why smart-contract vulnerabilities are especially costly.

The 95% problem

Fewer than 5% of deployed Ethereum contracts have published source code. Decompilation and de-obfuscation of bytecode remain open research areas.

AI and blockchain

Payments between AI agents, verifiable AI inference, proof of personhood, and the use of AI in finding smart-contract vulnerabilities.

Student pitch contest

Ten student teams present Ethereum projects, tools and research ideas to a jury.

Format: a 5-minute pitch, hard-timed, then 4 minutes of questions from the jury. Pitches run from 12:30 to 2:00 PM, after a 5-minute introduction.

Yash Singh

Third-year undergraduate, Department of Engineering Physics, IIT Indore

Invited talk · 11:20 – 11:45 AM

Yash spent about a year as a Founding Engineer at Tychi Labs, where he worked on Tychi Wallet (iOS and Android) and built the Universal Gas Framework, a multi-chain transaction infrastructure covering execution, relaying, simulation, signing and gas abstraction, which has processed more than 100,000 transactions. His current work centres on BNCK, a self-custodial financial system for people and AI agents, and Tychi Agent Finance, an open-source TypeScript SDK and MCP server for agent self-custody and policy-controlled spending, with support for x402 and ERC-8004. He also contributes to the Canton Network (Digital Asset) and Hyperledger Besu open-source projects.

Dr. Debasish Pattanayak

Assistant Professor, Department of CSE, IIT Indore

AI Agents: Life and Money · 2:00 – 2:40 PM

Dr. Pattanayak is a researcher in distributed computing, known for his work on distributed algorithms for mobile agents and robot swarms, including exploration, rendezvous, dispersion, pattern formation and fault tolerance. His work appears at leading venues such as DISC, SPAA, ICPP, IPDPS and ICDCN, and in journals including Distributed Computing, the Journal of Parallel and Distributed Computing and Theoretical Computer Science. He received his BTech and PhD from IIT Guwahati and held research positions at the University of Vienna, LUISS Guido Carli in Rome, the Université du Québec en Outaouais, Carleton University and the University of Ottawa before joining IIT Indore in 2025.
